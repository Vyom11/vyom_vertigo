## What they're doing now

Start with the front door. Sarvam's [homepage](https://www.sarvam.ai/) leads with "AI for all from India", followed by "Built on sovereign compute. Powered by frontier-class models." Below that sit speech-to-text, text-to-speech, translation and conversational agents across 22 Indian languages, and the enterprise lines stay short and declarative.

Then the pace, which is hard to miss. Sarvam [unveiled its 30B and 105B models on 18 February 2026](https://www.businesstoday.in/tech-today/story/sarvam-ai-launches-30b-and-105b-open-source-models-516729-2026-02-18) at the India AI Impact Summit, and [launched the Indus chat app in beta](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/20/indias-sarvam-launches-indus-ai-chat-app-as-competition-heats-up/) two days later, for web and mobile. Inc42 reports the model launch was part of a ["14 day-14 launch" programme](https://inc42.com/?p=549483).

The [blog](https://www.sarvam.ai/blogs) leans on one headline pattern: "Introducing [Product] [Version]" (Saaras V4 on 25 Sep, Bulbul V4 Flash on 13 Oct, Prism on 3 Nov 2026). The [Prism post](https://www.sarvam.ai/blogs/sarvam-prism) describes a decision model released with open weights under Apache 2.0, a median response of about 44 ms, and dense benchmark tables. Sarvam's own evaluations produced those figures.

The [Tata Capital story](https://www.sarvam.ai/stories/tata-capital-ai-voice-transformation) (18 Feb 2026) covers the Samvaad voice agent: English plus 10 Indian languages, with human agents taking nuanced calls. Its impact section speaks in general terms, and the one hard figure ("20L+" monthly interactions) appears only in the page's metadata.

On [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/sarvam-ai/posts/) (about 160K followers) I saw two items: a short announcement that Sashikumar Sreedharan joins as President and CBO, linking to the blog post, and a repost of an investor's long write-up of a conversation with co-founder Vivek Raghavan about owning versus renting intelligence. A [27 Oct post](https://www.sarvam.ai/blogs/sarvam-hq-ids-mou) announces an MoU with HQ IDS for AI projects across the three services, with a clarification added on 28 Oct about who signed.

## The gap

Credit first, because it's earned. Sarvam ships at a pace most startups only manage in a pitch deck, releases open weights, writes headlines you can read without a glossary, and picked voice as the front door for Indus, which is how India actually talks. (An engineer by degree, so yes, I read the Prism tables. Twice. Nobody asked me to.)

Now the trouble, and it's a casting problem. Sarvam has one voice and two audiences. The homepage speaks national pride. The Prism post speaks benchmark. The person who signs off a voice-agent budget gets neither a sentence nor a scene, and the two LinkedIn items I saw talk to the industry and to investors, not to that buyer. (Two items is a tiny sample, so hold it lightly.) My hypothesis: that reader bounces between the registers and leaves without ever meeting a customer.

Second, the proof is in the wrong room. The Tata Capital story is where a buyer goes looking for evidence, and its best number lives in the metadata, while the body opens on background. That's a film that opens with the studio logos. Nobody stays for the logos. The fix isn't inventing numbers. It's moving the approved one to the top.

Third, the headlines announce instead of invite. "Introducing Saaras V4" tells a returning developer everything and a new reader nothing. It's a poster that lists the cast and forgets the plot.

## The refinement

**Move 1: Benefit above the version number.**  
Put the line a human would say before the line an engineer would file. Sample for the Prism post, with the figures flagged as Sarvam's own:

> **Prism: when you need a decision, not a paragraph.**
>
> One pass. One probability per option. Median response about 44 ms, by our own tests.
>
> Most models talk before they answer. Prism skips the speech. You give it the options; it tells you how sure it is of each one. Built for enterprise workflows, where "fast and calibrated" beats "eloquent". (Our benchmarks, our hardware. Check them.)

Alternate headlines: *"Prism: the model that doesn't write essays."* / *"A decision model, not a chatbot."*

**Move 2: Open the customer story on a scene.**  
Illustrative scene, not a real call. The figure comes from Tata Capital-approved data, not from me.

> A customer calls and asks in her own language. The voice answers in it. When the question needs judgment, a human picks up.
>
> That's Samvaad at Tata Capital. [Lead number: confirm the "20L+ monthly interactions" figure with Tata Capital before using it here.]

Background and architecture follow after the reveal, not before it.

**Move 3: An Indus reel built for the thumb.**  
Per [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/artificial-intelligence/sarvam-launches-indus-chat-app-as-interface-for-its-ai-models), Indus is voice-first, supports 22 Indian languages and is in beta. I haven't tested it, so this is a script outline, not a product claim.

- **Hook (0-3s):** hands full in a kitchen, no screen visible. Line: *"Poochho. Jis bhasha mein bhi."*
- **(3-10s):** she asks something half in Hindi, half in English. The answer appears on screen and plays aloud.
- **(10-16s):** cut to a second person, a different language, same ease. No narration. *(Beat. Let the cut do the talking.)*
- **End card (16-20s):** "Indus by Sarvam. In beta. Bol ke dekho."

## How I'd measure it

All of this is **hypothesis**; I have no Sarvam baselines, and I'd rather say so than invent them.

- **H1:** Benefit-first headlines lift click-through to launch posts from non-developer visitors. Watch CTR by audience segment, scroll depth and time on page.
- **H2:** A scene-first customer story lifts "Contact us" clicks per story view versus the current format. Watch CTA click rate and story-to-form conversion.
- **H3:** Voice-led reels earn more saves and shares per view than product-feature clips. Watch saves, shares, 3-second and 10-second retention, and link clicks to Indus.

## Sign-off

*Benchmarks for the engineers. Bol-chaal for everyone else.*
