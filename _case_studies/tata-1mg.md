## What they're doing now

The [homepage](https://www.1mg.com/) is titled "Online Pharmacy India \| Buy Medicines from India's Trusted Medicine Store", and its content runs on offers: promo codes (extra 15% off a first in-app order), "Get in 30 mins" delivery labels, deal blocks such as "Limited time deals". Alongside sit the trust markers: verified, licensed pharmacies, privacy for doctor chats, LegitScript and ISO/IEC 27001:2022, plus stats (260m+ visitors, 31m+ orders). There's a "Sehat ka Sathi" tagline and a Hindi articles link in the navigation.

The [articles listing](https://www.1mg.com/articles/) is a different mood. Titles like ["Cold Symptoms Timeline: What Happens Each Day and When to Worry"](https://www.1mg.com/articles/cold-symptoms-timeline-what-happens-each-day-and-when-to-worry/) and ["Should You Skip Your Morning Walk When AQI Is Poor?"](https://www.1mg.com/articles/should-you-skip-your-morning-walk-when-aqi-is-poor/) sit on cards showing author, date and category tags, with a sidebar inviting an online doctor consultation.

On [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/1mgofficialpage/) the account has about 88k followers and 2,017 posts. The bio calls it "India's digital health platform" and talks of understandable, accessible and affordable care, with meds in 30-60 minutes. Highlights include The 1mg Show, AMA, AMA - PCOS, Diabetes Day and 9DaysOfWellness. The recent grid I could read is mostly education carousels and reels: a diabetes-diet guide, festive eating, pet safety at Diwali, why a gut can change across a menstrual cycle, precautions on high-AQI days, acne as a possible signal, sugar alternatives. (Captions and thumbnails only; I did not read the slides.)

Then the campaign that got the trophy. Tata 1mg won a [Bronze Lion in the Pharma category at Cannes Lions 2026](https://www.mediainfoline.com/awards/tata-1mg-wins-bronze-lion-at-cannes-lions-2026-for-its-sawaal-uthao-campaign) for "Sawaal Uthao". The idea is to get shoppers asking whether their medicine is genuine. Made with the agency Humour Me, it uses Track & Trace QR codes on medicine strips sold through the platform; scanning one in the app shows the medicine's journey from sourcing and storage to delivery. Earlier, Tata 1mg Labs launched ["Trust What You See"](https://www.mediainfoline.com/tag/tata-1mg-labs) with dentsu X India (2023) to highlight the accuracy and reliability of its diagnostics. One outside estimate: ad-tracker Hawky counted [1,595 active Tata 1mg ads between June 2025 and June 2026](https://hawky.ai/blog/decoding-tata-1mg), 1,248 on Google and 347 on Meta, with a Full Body Health Checkup image ad the longest-running top creative. Those are an outside tool's counts, not the brand's.

## The gap

Credit first. The trust architecture is real (licensing, traceability), the article titles read like service journalism, and Hindi sits in the main navigation. Few pharmacies can say "scan the strip" and mean it.

So why does the best idea sit in an award entry? I'd put it this way: Tata 1mg speaks in two voices, and the award winner is in neither. The homepage text I read runs on deals and countdowns. Instagram runs on save-worthy education. Sawaal Uthao, which speaks in questions, didn't show up in the homepage text or in the recent Instagram grid and highlights I could read, though it may live elsewhere. It's a lead performance with no screen time.

Second, a smaller question: who checked this? The articles listing shows author and date but no reviewer line. Article pages may carry one. In health, who vouched for the page is part of the headline.

Third, a thread left half-pulled. If the Hawky read is right that checkups carry the longest-running creative, the "Trust What You See" line has room to travel across more of it. (Hypothesis, built on a third-party estimate.)

## The refinement

**Move 1: Turn "Sawaal Uthao" into a weekly format.**  
Their feed already runs carousels people save, so this slots in. "Ek Sawaal, Ek Strip" uses only what the Cannes coverage states about the QR feature.

> **Slide 1:** Yeh dawai asli hai? Poochna jaayaz hai.  
> **Slide 2:** Track & Trace QR wali strip scan karo. App mein dikhegi iski journey: sourcing se delivery tak.  
> **Slide 3:** Sawaal poochna rude nahi hai. Samajhdaari hai.  
> **Slide 4:** Agla sawaal: [rotating question, written with the medical team].  
> **Slide 5:** Scan karo. Phir bharosa karo.

Caption: *"Sawaal uthao. Jawaab hum dikhayenge."*

**Move 2: One reviewer line, one closing block.**  
Hooks for existing article topics, kept free of medical claims:

- Smog-season eyes: *"Aankhein jal rahi hain? Pehle yeh padho. Phir Google karo."*
- Air purifiers: *"Purifier chal raha hai. Par kya woh aapke kamre ke liye hai?"*

Structure for every article, with the content filled by their doctors: **"Reviewed by [name, qualification, date]"** under the headline, and a standard closing block, **"Doctor se kab milein"**.

**Move 3: A checkup headline set.**  
Built on the brand's own "Trust What You See" thread. Offer lines to be pulled from the live listing.

> Trust what you see. Book the test.  
> Pehle jaancho. Phir socho.  
> One checkup. Real numbers. [Offer line per live listing.]

## How I'd measure it

All **hypotheses**; I have no Tata 1mg baselines.

- **H1:** The Sawaal series earns more saves and shares per view than deal-led carousels, and drives clicks to the authenticity explainer. Watch saves, shares and link clicks.
- **H2:** A visible reviewer line plus a "Doctor se kab milein" block lifts scroll depth and clicks to the online consultation. Watch scroll depth and consult-click rate from article pages.
- **H3:** Trust-led checkup headlines match or beat deal-led headlines on click-through to booking. Watch CTR and booking conversion in a split test.

## Sign-off

*Ask first. Trust second.*
