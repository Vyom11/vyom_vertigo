We are a land of ideas. By *we*, I mean the nation of India.

We want to achieve great things. We aspire to have education like Norway, the civility of Singapore, the opportunities of the United States, but the mentality should remain Indian.

Now, when I say mentality, to me that meant being welcoming, respectful to all whilst also retaining the idea that we were no less than anyone.

Now that mentality has been distilled to a single sentence: **“Hum toh aise hi hain.”**

And the rush for change that made this nation put its stamp on the world is now content with simply going along with the flow. And the flow is not of a serene river that flows beautifully. It is industrial waste that will pollute the surroundings and eat us from the inside out.

We as Indians have started to romanticise our suffering as a means to cope with the realities of the nation.

A place has dilapidated structures but is surrounded by wild trees? We think of it as being surrounded by nature.

Rape, murder, loot happens? We put in stories of some other woman achieving great things. Or, if you want to go in the opposite direction, we put in stories of women who share their views openly, only for our learned sages to call it a “cultural shock”.

We see a beggar sleeping under the shade. We romanticise how the best feeling is to sleep under a shade, while typing the caption from our rosy AC rooms.

Our hope of demanding public decency and good living is a dream that would even put my dream of being an actor to shame.

The only way to describe it would be as **“sapne suhane Ladakh pan ke.”**

We are so used to the frequent power cuts, bad air quality, poor roads, bad infrastructure and lack of opportunities that we find ways around them, not to fix them, but just to forget about them.

Power cuts are there? Pay more money and get into a gated society.

Air quality is bad? Get a Chinese air purifier.

After all, citizenship of India is subject to conditioning.

If roads are bad, then there is no need to go out. Let's have a minimum-wage worker come to us and call it the “gig economy”.

Lack of opportunities are there? Well, we will just go to a foreign nation and settle there while putting up a status saying *Maa Tujhe Salaam.*

And all this is due to the fact that India is not a nation. It is an idea of a nation that can't figure out what it wants to be.

And that is a blessing and a curse, depending on who you ask.

Because no other nation exists in this world that is democratic in nature, secular by design, while being surrounded by nations that are either idiotic by design, smart but controlling in nature, or simply unpredictable.

Even with all this, we stand proud.

Maybe we are standing because we are peeing outside.

But hey, it's natural fertiliser, as some would say.

Yet, amidst all this hullabaloo, I am confident.

After all, I am a romantic at heart.

India means something to people. I know it does. And perhaps, someday, that change will come.

This nation was not handed to us on a platter. People far greater than me sacrificed everything so that I could write this essay wearing a China-made T-shirt, using an American laptop, with an Indian mind.

Which one is worth more is a little blurry these days.

**Happy Independence Day to this great nation.**
