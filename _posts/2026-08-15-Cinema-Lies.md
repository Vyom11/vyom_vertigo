I was reading my way through the book *Ten Classics* by Anitaa Padhye and, as the name suggests, it consists of tidbits from well-known classics of Indian cinema. This was to prepare for my YouTube series titled *The Classics*, where I review these films and share my thoughts on them. And this is where it hit me: **cinema as a medium will never make any change.**

The book starts by talking about the film *Do Bigha Zamin*, and how a lowly farmer has to fight for the land that was taken away from him by the deceit of landlords. Now, the film was a smashing hit in theatres, though, to be fair, during the first few days the response was tepid as people thought the ending was a bit too dire and saddening. After the ending was changed, the movie went on to become a major hit.

Now, that film should have started talks about how we treat people we perceive as “lower-class”. But you visit any street in India and you will see the class divide in full display. Next time you board an AC bus and see a person in traditional clothing, look at how other people frown upon them. Or next time you go out house hunting, meet new people, and bond over a similar topic of interest, notice the questions that would be asked of you. They will answer the question by themselves.

This incessant need to feel superior to others, even though we ourselves have not achieved much. **The nation has, we have not.**

You will find countless stories of India achieving great things in the world of engineering, the medical field, renewable energy and countless other areas. But if *Do Bigha Zamin* had actually made a difference, we would have tried to create a better outcome for our society as a whole.

Every day I sit in an AC bus and watch people sleeping below flyovers because that is the only respite from heat and rain that they can get. When I drop off, I walk amongst skyscrapers that strain my neck to its limit, though it's at this moment that I am hit by a scooty coming from the wrong side on the footpath.

Finally reaching the office, I am met with companies that are earning millions in revenue, while the stairs are filled with tobacco spit marks. Though that could be called art, so you never know.

India really is a classic case of an unemployed youth who starts daydreaming about the future the minute they apply for a job, though working for it… there's the rub.

But fine, *Do Bigha Zamin* was released in 1953. It's an old film.

You take any recent examples, say films like *3 Idiots*, *Article 15*, *Ship of Theseus*. All these movies have something profound to say about the nature of Indian society, psychology and behaviour.

**Do you see any change in Indian society?**

I personally don't.

Sure, we quote the dialogues on casteism, sing the songs in every engineering first-year orientation, quote *Ship of Theseus* verbatim for a debate, but the actual change in your behaviour, well, that remains to be seen.

Now, I am not saying India is uniquely guilty of this. But I can only write from my perspective and experiences. I am not smart enough to leave another country, so I am stuck looking at Indians, though the image is blurry due to the high AQI.

And in today's age, this is especially depressing because the advent of AI, excess information and the internet have made us so desensitised to any form of news that it really is shocking.

Rape, murder, loot, joy, anger, sadness — these have become fleeting moments that flow faster than a speeding bullet. Maybe that is the real problem. Everything arrives, everything shocks us, and everything disappears just as quickly.

Real, human-made art is probably one of the few things left that can make us actually feel something in our hearts.

Art, poetry, dance, music, film, theatre — all have the capability to change worlds, move mountains, create feelings beyond measure.

The only thing remaining is the heart to listen to it and the mind to act upon it.

But my heart is busy listening to *Loser* by Tame Impala, and my mind is already occupied with thoughts of what I'll do at a million followers.
