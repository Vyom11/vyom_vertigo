# Style notes: Vyom's voice, as observed in the repo

Working document. Not part of the published site (excluded in `_config.yml`).
Sources read: `/about/`, all 5 case studies, `/writing/` index, and the essays
*Introducing Vyom Vertigo*, *From Creators to Mimics*, *India. From Hopes to Reality*,
*Do Engineering*, plus openings of *AI and its place in our world* and *How Cinema Lies*.

## What the repo confirms

1. **Short lines doing the heavy lifting.** One-sentence paragraphs as a beat, then a longer one.
   > "Because that is ultimately what this is."
   > "Lights down. Enjoy the show."
2. **Self-deprecation by parenthesis or a shrug.**
   > "(Fortunately, *abba maan gaye*.)"
   > "But hey, I am just a guy rambling on the internet, so you do you."
3. **Film references as the native language of an argument.**
   > "...even *Wall-E* would commit seppuku seeing what we are doing with the world"
   > "Like everyone, I did engineering. Like Farhan, I chose the passion over the placement."
4. **Hinglish dropped in, never glossed.**
   > "**'Hum toh aise hi hain.'**"  /  "**'sapne suhane Ladakh pan ke.'**"
5. **Craft vocabulary from magic and theatre, applied to work.**
   > "Misdirection, timing and the reveal turn out to be a good copywriting education."
6. **A flat, dry rule-of-three or A/B with a sting in the tail.**
   > "Power cuts are there? Pay more money and get into a gated society. / Air quality is bad? Get a Chinese air purifier."
   > "some people go to eat a cuisine that is painstakingly made, and some simply enjoy pre-processed chips for dinner."
7. **Sign-offs are small and personal, not slogans.**
   > "**PS:** No AI was used while writing this essay."  /  "human-made, machine-literate."  /  "Ciao."

## Where the repo differs from the brief (worth knowing)

- **Essays run conversational and long-breathed**, more than clipped. The punchy fragments are
  the seasoning, not the base. I will keep fragments as contrast, not as a tic.
- **Case-study pages are plainer than the essays.** They are warm, specific and light on jokes
  (one real gag: "Plot twist: written by a strict coffee person."). The teardowns can be more playful
  than the existing five, but I'll keep the humour to one or two beats per page, because these are
  pitched to brands.
- **Em dashes appear in the essays**, used singly. I'll use them rarely and never stacked, per your rule.
- **Colon-led titles are not in your essay titles yet** ("Do Engineering", "How Cinema Lies").
  They do appear in subtitles. I'll use the colon form for section titles inside teardowns.
- **Opinions are stated, then softened with a wink** ("Fight me" energy lives in About; essays say
  "I find that so depressing", "to each their own"). For brands I'll be opinionated about craft,
  never about people or the company.
- The existing pages say **"Role / Industry / Read"** in the header and a results band of numbers.
  Spec teardowns have no numbers to claim, so the band will say "Spec teardown" instead.

## Rules I'll write by

- Craft over claims. Every line of sample copy should be specific to that brand, or it gets cut.
- No filler, no listicle padding, no motivational ending.
- Fair first: name what the brand does well before any gap.
- Facts only from public sources I can open and link. Projections are labelled "hypothesis".
- Each piece ends on a small sign-off line in the spirit of "human-made, machine-literate".
